- Phase 16 extends the same notebook with a controlled initialization / convergence-map diagnostic, again on the exact frozen Phase 9 surrogate derivative field and the original `MinimalSymNet` only. The objective on September 3, 2026 was not to fix SymNet but to test whether deliberately structured initializations converge in systematic ways and whether there is a visible separation between starts that return to the Burgers-like / least-squares basin and starts that do not. The preserved reference points are `theta_good = theta_ref`, the earlier Burgers-like frozen-SymNet point with PDE MSE `8.995417e-02`; `theta_bad`, the known bad random-final frozen-SymNet point with PDE MSE `9.651142e-01`; `xi_true`, the true Burgers coefficient vector; and `xi_LS`, the Phase 11 least-squares coefficient vector on the frozen surrogate field. The notebook reused the same return-to-good criterion as Phase 15: `final PDE MSE <= 9.429386e-02` and `final ||xi - xi_LS||_2 <= 1.162858e-02`.

For Experiment A, the straight parameter-space interpolation `theta(alpha) = (1 - alpha) * theta_good + alpha * theta_bad` with `alpha = 0.0, 0.1, ..., 1.0` produced the clearest controlled split. Starts at `alpha = 0.0`, `0.1`, `0.8`, and `0.9` returned to the good basin, finishing near `coeff(u*u_x) approx -0.89`, `coeff(u_xx) approx +0.010`, and PDE MSE `8.98e-02` to `9.19e-02`. Starts at `alpha = 0.2` through `0.7` and the exact bad endpoint `alpha = 1.0` did not. Their final states formed a systematic low-transport / weakened-diffusion band rather than random scatter: `alpha = 0.2` ended at PDE MSE `1.02849e-01`, `coeff(u*u_x) = -0.764664`, `coeff(u_xx) = +0.008818`, distance `0.142003`; `alpha = 0.3` through `0.7` degraded progressively to PDE MSE `8.84349e-01` with `coeff(u*u_x) = -0.003324`, `coeff(u_xx) = +0.002751`, distance `0.896761`; and `alpha = 1.0` ended at PDE MSE `6.93044e-01`, `coeff(u*u_x) = -0.040230`, `coeff(u_xx) = -0.004196`, distance `0.896498`. The observed split is therefore real but non-monotone in `alpha`, which is consistent with the nonlinear, redundant `theta -> xi` map: straight lines in parameter space do not induce simple monotone progress in coefficient space.

For Experiment B, the product pathway was weakened by scaling only the `product_readout` parameter while leaving the left-factor weights, right-factor weights, and linear path fixed. This directly scales the entire quadratic block generated by the one product unit. Recovery was much more robust than the interpolation experiment suggested. Product scales `1.0`, `0.75`, `0.5`, `0.1`, and `0.0` all returned near the good basin, with final PDE MSE between `8.9804e-02` and `9.1277e-02` and final coefficient distance to `xi_LS` between `5.37e-03` and `6.98e-03`. The only threshold miss under the strict Phase 15 return rule was `product_scale = 0.25`, whose final PDE MSE was slightly high at `9.5917e-02` even though its final coefficients were still very close to the good region (`coeff(u*u_x) = -0.891866`, `coeff(u_xx) = +0.009949`, distance `5.242e-03`). Current read: suppressing the nonlinear amplitude alone does not force the model into the bad random-start basin.

For Experiment C, the diffusion pathway was weakened by scaling only the linear `u_xx` parameter in `theta_good`. This family was even more stable. Every tested diffusion scale from `1.0` down to `0.0` returned to essentially the same final point, with final PDE MSE `8.9804e-02`, final `coeff(u*u_x) = -0.894597`, final `coeff(u_xx) = +0.010185`, and final `||xi - xi_LS||_2 = 5.815e-03` up to displayed precision. That means this local basin is not very sensitive to removing the initial diffusion contribution when the rest of the good parameter support is left intact.

For Experiment D, hand-constructed wrong-sign / wrong-magnitude initial equations were also corrected robustly. The tested starts were `+1.0 * u*u_x + 0.02 * u_xx`, `-0.5 * u*u_x + 0.02 * u_xx`, `-0.25 * u*u_x + 0.02 * u_xx`, `-1.0 * u*u_x - 0.02 * u_xx`, and `-1.0 * u*u_x + 0.00 * u_xx`, each verified before training by its expanded coefficient vector. All five converged back to the good basin with final PDE MSE `8.9804e-02` and final coefficient distances near `5.8e-03`. Even the wrong-sign transport case `+1.0 * u*u_x + 0.02 * u_xx`, which started at PDE MSE `7.962343`, ended at `coeff(u*u_x) = -0.894571` and `coeff(u_xx) = +0.010185`.

The new primary visualization is the coefficient-plane trajectory plot with axes `(coeff(u*u_x), coeff(u_xx))`, marking the true Burgers point `(-1, 0.02)` and the Phase 11 least-squares point. This was the most informative plot of the phase. It showed the interpolation family splitting into two practical behaviors under identical optimization: one subset of starts moved toward the compact Burgers-like / `xi_LS` region, while another subset bent toward a common low-transport band with smaller `|coeff(u*u_x)|` and smaller `coeff(u_xx)`. By contrast, the product-weakening, diffusion-weakening, and wrong-sign families all showed trajectories returning to nearly the same final region despite large differences in their initial coefficients.

Current interpretation: initialization controls SymNet recovery strongly and systematically, but the decisive variable is not merely the scalar sign or magnitude of `coeff(u*u_x)` or `coeff(u_xx)` alone. If the initialization stays on the simple hand-constructed Burgers support, the optimizer corrects even severe sign and amplitude mistakes. The harder failures arise from broader parameter-space displacement toward the bad random-start region, as exposed by the good-to-bad interpolation family. The conservative conclusion is therefore that there is a visible basin split in this diagnostic, with failed runs tending toward a related low-transport family of bad solutions, and that full parameter-space initialization geometry matters much more than isolated Burgers-coefficient sign errors. The next open question is how to build an initialization or parameterization that makes the good coefficient-aligned region reachable from generic starts without hand construction.

The scaled run ended with full-grid data MSE `2.594081e-02`, PDE loss `9.076120e-03`, physical `u*u_x=-0.083009`, `u_xx=0.006308`, and coefficient error `0.942280`; the unscaled run ended with data MSE `7.360549e-02`, PDE loss `9.479609e+02`, `u*u_x=-0.222421`, `u_xx=0.396497`, and coefficient error `1.122056`. Pre-joint derivative MSEs were `u_x=7.615923e-02` and `u_xx=2.657628e+02`; post-joint values were `u_x=4.100288`, `u_xx=2.522940e+03` unscaled and `u_x=2.100481`, `u_xx=2.097570e+03` scaled. Both joint runs visibly deformed the pretrained surrogate, but fixed scaling reduced the measured data and derivative drift and avoided the unscaled PDE blow-up in this single controlled run. It improved joint PDE convergence and moved coefficients in the right direction, though it did not recover the Burgers transport coefficient. No multi-seed claim is made; Phase 19B should test pretrained-state and seed reliability while preserving these controls.

- Phase 19B (2026-09-09) tested joint training from scratch with no surrogate pretraining. One predetermined seed (`19019`) initialized a SIREN and `MinimalSymNet`; exact clones were used for raw, fixed-initial-RMS, and dynamic-detached-RMS conditions. All used Adam `lr=5e-4`, 1,000 minibatch epochs, `batch_size=4096`, `lambda_data=1.0`, `lambda_pde=0.5`, identical sampled batches, and no regularization. Initial random-surrogate RMS scales were `u=8.515415e-01`, `u_x=5.586467e+00`, `u_xx=8.552398e+01`. The dynamic scale used detached minibatch RMS with EMA `alpha=0.99`, updated once per batch. Final data/PDE losses were raw `0.638230/4.468448`, fixed `0.033361/0.021038`, and dynamic `0.032683/0.020318`; final physical coefficients `(u*u_x, u_xx)` were raw `(-0.044208, 0.287210)`, fixed `(-0.021400, 0.002970)`, and dynamic `(-0.083696, 0.005158)`, with coefficient errors `1.249097`, `1.005353`, and `0.960624`. Dynamic running scales ended at `u=0.827198`, `u_x=1.675292`, `u_xx=19.187775`; their observed min/max ranges were `0.522606–0.851542`, `1.675292–5.586467`, and `19.187775–85.523979`, respectively. Full-grid RMS snapshots showed the initial fixed scale becoming stale: by epoch 1000 the raw surrogate RMS ratios were approximately `0.30`, `0.14`, and `0.09` for `u`, `u_x`, and `u_xx`, while the fixed and dynamic models' field RMS values moved substantially during learning. Predetermined data-loss thresholds (`1e-1`, `1e-2`, `1e-3`) were crossed at epoch `500`, never, never for fixed and dynamic at the retained checkpoints; raw crossed none. Dynamic scaling therefore improved the from-scratch PDE objective and physical recovery relative to raw, but it did not recover the Burgers transport coefficient and did not approach the Phase 19A pretrained fixed-scale result (`data MSE 2.594081e-02`, `PDE 9.076120e-03`, coefficient error `0.942280`) decisively. This single-seed result supports dynamic normalization as a useful optimization control, not as evidence that pretraining can be eliminated generally. Remaining uncertainty is seed and horizon reliability; the natural next experiment is a predeclared multi-seed comparison of from-scratch dynamic scaling against the pretrained fixed-scale control.
